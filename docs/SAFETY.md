# PackForge Safety Protocol & Experimental Boundary

---

## 1. Safety Notice & Legal Disclaimer

PackForge is a **virtual design exploration and trade-off synthesis tool**, NOT an operational safety case or manufacturing blueprint.
- Designing and assembling physical lithium-ion battery packs carries significant hazards including electrical shock, arc flash, toxic gas release, and catastrophic thermal runaway.
- This repository contains **zero instructions** for building hazardous high-voltage hardware.
- No design generated by this platform should be assembled without formal engineering sign-off from qualified battery systems engineers and compliance with applicable electrical safety regulations.

---

## 2. Low-Voltage Boundary: Extra-Low Voltage (ELV) Rule

PackForge enforces a hard requirement (**Requirement R-02**) restricting pack maximum voltage to:
$$V_{\text{pack,max}} \le 60.0\text{ V DC}$$
- In international electrical codes (IEC 60364-4-41), voltages below 60 V DC are classified as **Safety Extra-Low Voltage (SELV)**.
- Operating within SELV minimizes dangerous shock hazards during bench experimentation, removing the need for high-voltage interlock loops (HVIL) or specialized high-voltage isolation gear.

---

## 3. Laboratory Testing Protocols

Any physical validation suggested in PackForge Design Dossiers must strictly adhere to the following safety protocols:
1. **Low-Voltage Submodule Scale**: Prototype testing should be conducted only on single cells or low-count parallel submodules (e.g., 1S4P) under supervised laboratory conditions.
2. **Current-Limited Power Supplies**: All charge and discharge cyclers must feature redundant hardware over-current and over-voltage trips.
3. **Thermal Chamber Protections**: Cell cyclers must be placed inside fire-rated, exhaust-ventilated explosion chambers equipped with Class-D fire extinguishers.
4. **PPE Requirements**: Technicians must wear flame-retardant lab coats, face shields, and insulated safety gloves during all terminal connections.
